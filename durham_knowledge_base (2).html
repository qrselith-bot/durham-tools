Your Authority

What you are trusted and empowered to do on your own — and where to draw the line.

🔑

The Durham Philosophy

You are trusted to make decisions that serve the guest. Bias toward the guest experience. If a situation calls for action and you believe it is the right move, do it. You will not be penalized for a good-faith call made in a guest's favor. Act first, ask questions after, and document everything in the pass-on. The only thing that requires a pause is money.

Quick decision guide

Not sure what to do? Ask these three questions.

1
Is anyone in immediate danger or distress?
Medical emergency, fire, threatening behavior, or guest safety concern.
Yes — Call 911 immediately, then GMNo — continue to question 2
2
Does resolving this require adjusting or refunding money?
Rate changes, discounts, refunds, or altering an OTA reservation.
Yes — Pause and contact managementNo — you are empowered to handle it
3
Can you make the guest feel genuinely taken care of right now?
A room move, a token, a voucher, an upgrade, or an honest explanation.
Yes — Do it. Log it. Move on.Not sure — use a phrase from Scenarios, then escalate if needed
What you can do on your own
✓ Solo — no call needed

Room Changes & Upgrades

  • Move a guest to any room for any valid reason
  • Upgrade to a higher category if inventory allows
  • Block a room for a guest with a strong preference
  • Reassign due to noise, maintenance, or guest request
✓ Solo — no call needed

Guest Recovery (no money)

  • Offer drink tokens at the rooftop bar
  • Offer a breakfast voucher — one entree plus non-alcoholic beverage
  • Complimentary water, coffee, or amenity items at the desk
  • Waive a late checkout fee
  • Grant early check-in when a clean room is confirmed
✓ Solo — no call needed

Operational Calls

  • Send maintenance to any room for a guest-reported issue
  • Issue new or duplicate key cards (ID required every time)
  • Handle luggage storage requests
  • Respond to noise complaints between guests
  • Assist with local recommendations or restaurant bookings
⚡ Use judgment — then log it

Gray Area Situations

  • Guest is very upset — do what you can, document everything
  • Room not ready at check-in — upgrade or offer an amenity
  • Guest claims pre-existing room damage
  • Walk situation developing — start the process, notify management at the same time
✗ Pause — contact management

Requires Approval

  • Rate adjustments or discounts after check-in
  • Partial or full night refunds
  • Expedia Collect reservation alterations of any kind
  • Comping an entire night's stay
  • Any charge removal from a folio
✗ Always escalate

Escalate Immediately

  • Medical emergency — call 911 first, GM second
  • Law enforcement on property or requested
  • Guest threatening staff or another guest
  • Suspected trafficking or illegal activity
  • Media or press inquiry — never comment, refer to GM always
If you cannot reach management
📲

Escalation Protocol

Try to reach Eli directly. If unreachable, give the guest Eli's direct contact information and assure them management will follow up personally. Log the full situation in the pass-on — what happened, what you offered, how the guest responded, and the time.

Situations

Room is not ready at check-in

Guest Recovery▼
The situation

Guest arrives at or after 3pm and their room has not been cleaned yet.

Your instinct

Apologize genuinely and offer something tangible. Do not over-explain or make excuses.

What to do

Check HK status in SNT Offer upgrade if available Offer drink token Store luggage if needed

Give an honest time estimate. If more than 30 minutes past 3pm, an upgrade or drink is appropriate and entirely your call. Log it in the pass-on.

Noise complaint from a guest

Guest Recovery▼
The situation

A guest calls or comes to the desk upset about noise from a neighboring room or the hallway.

Your instinct

Take it seriously. A guest who cannot sleep is a problem you can solve right now.

What to do

Call the noisy room politely Offer to move the complaining guest Follow up with a second call if needed

If sleep was genuinely disrupted, a breakfast voucher or drink token is appropriate and entirely your call. No call needed. Log it.

We need to walk a guest

Escalate + Act▼
The situation

The hotel is fully committed and a guest needs to be relocated to another property.

Your instinct

Start the process immediately. Do not freeze waiting for instructions. Notify management at the same time, not before.

What to do

Call 21C, Marriott, Aloft, JB Duke, Washington Duke Ask for walk rate and room hold Pick best guest to walk (1-night, lower rate, non-repeat) Contact management now

The financial side is management's call. Your job is to have a room secured before the guest arrives at the desk.

Guest disputes a charge at checkout

Needs management (if $)▼
The situation

Guest reviews their folio and questions a charge — incidentals, room service, rate discrepancy, or an unknown package.

Your instinct

Do not get defensive. Listen first. Most disputes are honest confusion, not bad faith.

What to do

Pull up the folio together Walk through each line clearly Explain incidental hold and how it releases

If a charge is genuinely wrong, loop in management before removing anything. Tell them: “I want to make sure this is resolved correctly — let me bring in my manager.”

Guest locked out or lost their key

Solo▼
What to do

Verify ID against reservation — no exceptions New key = deactivates the old one Duplicate key = both old and new stay active

Ask whether they lost the card or just need a spare. That determines which type to issue.

No-show guest — do we charge?

Follow SOP▼
What to do

Check no-show report after 3am during Audit Charge per reservation policy Log in pass-on

If the reservation is OTA, corporate, or has unusual circumstances, flag it for management rather than guessing. Charging incorrectly is harder to undo than a short delay.

Guest wants to split the bill at checkout

Solo▼
What to do

Use Nights vs. Amounts split in SNT Confirm which card covers which portion first Authorize new card before removing old one

Take your time, confirm the split clearly before processing, and make sure both cards go through successfully.

Procedures & Scripts

Reference for common processes, guest-facing language, and system workflows.

Guest communication

Pre-arrival call

"This is [name] calling from The Durham Hotel. I am calling to confirm your stay on [date] for a [#]-night visit. Check-in is 3pm and checkout is noon. If you would like to request an early arrival I am happy to note that."

On voicemail add: "Please call us back at 919-768-8830. Thank you for choosing The Durham."

Confirm during the call: number of nights, room type, number of guests, special requests, and any closures that may affect the stay.

Check-in

  • Welcome and confirm the reservation
  • Verify ID and take card for incidentals at $100 per night
  • Mention Coffee Bar and Restaurant hours
  • Mention Roof Bar and Patio
  • Mention coffee delivery service
  • Fitness center and YMCA passes
  • Mezz Lounge and business center
  • Issue keys — ask how many guests need cards

Check-out

  • Confirm guest email to send folio
  • Confirm phone number on file
  • Ask if they would like a printed copy of their folio
  • Offer complimentary bottles of water
  • Confirm the card on file is the correct one to close the bill
Third-party reservations

Hotel Collect (Expedia)

The guest pays us directly and we pay Expedia commission after. The guest's personal credit card is on file. These reservations can be adjusted or changed with management approval.

Expedia Collect

The guest paid Expedia directly before arriving. A virtual card is on file — not the guest's personal card. Do not alter or cancel without consulting management directly. The virtual card cannot be charged differently.

Cashier and payments

Cash payment — room and tax

  • Locate the yellow Cashier's Deposit Report slip
  • Take the amount and make change as needed
  • Fill out all information on the slip
  • Place in safe inside the cash drawer
  • Note: SNT shows as cash but the amount is not applied until paid in the Bill and Charges screen

Cash incidentals

  • Take $100 per night
  • Place in a Cashier's Deposit Report slip marked "incidentals"
  • Note the cash payment in the pass-on
  • Room must be inspected by housekeeping before returning the amount
  • This is not a charge and will not appear on the folio
Packages

Breakfast Package — $10 per night per person

  • One voucher per night — one entree plus one non-alcoholic beverage at breakfast
  • Vouchers have an expiration date and are not valid after checkout
  • Not redeemable for cash under any circumstance
  • Guests may add at check-in for $10 per person

Night Out Package — $15 per night

  • Guest receives a $25 credit at all F&B outlets including Coffee Bar, Rooftop, and Room Service
  • Unused credits are forfeited at checkout with no reimbursement
  • Credit applies across all outlets, not rooftop only
Room service

Hours and process

Breakfast: 7:30am to 11:00am     Afternoon and evening: 11:00am to 9:00pm

Fill out the room service ticket in the front desk binder (separated by dividers) with guest info and items ordered. For special requests, confirm with Restaurant or Rooftop before confirming with the guest. Additional forms are available as an Excel file on the front desk terminals.

Upgrade notes
⬆️

Offering an upgrade

Always check SNT availability first. Upgrades go to a cleaner, larger, or higher-floor room. You do not need management approval to upgrade a guest as long as the room is available and clean. Log it in the pass-on with a brief note on why.

Standard upgrade path when balancing inventory: DKO → DK → SK → SDD or SKS. Never switch kings to doubles or doubles to kings. Upgrading to ADA rooms is a last resort only.
